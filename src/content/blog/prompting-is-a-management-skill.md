This one's still rough — publishing it as a draft so it renders locally without going out over the newsletter yet.

The working thesis: the people who write the best prompts are, almost without exception, the people who were already good at writing a clear ticket, a clear brief, or a clear delegation to a new hire. Prompting didn't teach them clarity. They already had it, and prompting just gave them a faster feedback loop for practicing it.

If that's true, the advice "learn to prompt better" is aiming at the wrong layer. The actual skill to teach is: how do you specify an outcome precisely enough that someone (or something) without your context can hit it on the first try?
